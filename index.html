Long Jump Strength Program

Eight weeks of offseason lifting, then two short lifts a week around Friday meets.

How it works

Lift Monday, Wednesday and Friday for 8 weeks. Weeks 1–4 build strength; weeks 5–8 turn it into speed and pop off the board. Once meets start, drop to two short lifts a week.

Do plyos and sprints at the start of practice, before you lift, while you're fresh.

How hard to push (RPE). Effort is rated 1–10. RPE 7 means you could have done about 3 more good reps. RPE 8 means about 2 more. Nobody grinds to failure.

Warm up every time (10 minutes): light jog or skips, leg swings, walking lunges with a reach, glute bridges, A-skips, then 2 light sets of your first lift.

Weeks 1–4: Build the base

Moderate weights, more reps. Learn the lifts and build muscle, tendons and work capacity.

Plyos & sprints

2–3 days a week, right after the warm-up and before lifting. Rest fully between reps. A “contact” is every time a foot hits the ground.

  • Stop the set when landings get loud or sloppy.
  • On a big jumping day, skip box jumps in the lift.

  • Championship meets: last heavy lift 7–10 days before. The final week is Lift 2 at half the sets, or no lifting.

Adding weight

  • Add 5–10 lb to leg lifts and 5 lb to upper-body lifts when every rep looked clean at the target effort.
  • If form broke down, keep the same weight next week.
  • First-year lifters: stay with goblet squats, trap bar and lighter variations all offseason.

Staying healthy

  • Technique before weight, always. Use a spotter on every squat and bench set.
  • Tell your coach about pain below the kneecap, in the shins, or in the heel or Achilles. Jumping volume gets cut first.
  • Sleep and eat enough. Tired, underfed athletes don't get stronger.
  • Pain that changes how you move means stop and see the athletic trainer.
